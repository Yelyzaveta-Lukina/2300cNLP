      How it works

      Skip-gram training

      For every word in the corpus, the model learns to predict the words around it (a window of ±5). Negative sampling turns this into a cheap binary task: tell real context words from random “noise” words. Words used in similar contexts end up with similar vectors.

      Cosine similarity

      Every vector is normalised to length 1, so the dot product a·b equals the cosine of the angle between them. The 10 closest words are the ones with the highest cosine (1 = same direction, 0 = unrelated).

      Projecting to 2D / 3D

      PCA finds the directions of greatest variance among the plotted vectors and keeps the top 2 or 3. The axis labels show how much variance each keeps; the rest is lost, so distances on screen are only approximate. The list on the left is the exact ranking.
